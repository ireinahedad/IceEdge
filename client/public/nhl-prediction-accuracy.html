The short answer

ArakIce picked the winner of 59.5% of NHL games in an out-of-sample test covering 4,304 games from February 2023 to April 2026 (2,559 correct). For comparison, always picking the home team would have been right 54.0% of the time over the same games. These figures come from games the model was never trained on, which is the only honest way to measure a prediction model.

A hit rate near 60% is what a sound NHL model looks like. Hockey is one of the most random major sports: one bounce or one hot goaltender decides many games, and no model can honestly claim to pick 70% or 80% of winners over a full season. Treat any service that advertises numbers like that with suspicion.

Calibration: can you trust the percentage?

Picking winners is only half the job. For a bettor, what matters is whether the probability itself is right: when ArakIce says a team has a 55% chance, that team should win about 55% of the time. This is called calibration, and it is what lets you compare the model's number with a sportsbook's price.

Across the 4,304 test games, the average gap between ArakIce's predicted probability and the observed win rate was 2.1 percentage points (expected calibration error 0.021). Here is the full breakdown, grouped by the probability the model gave the home team:

Other measures

For readers who want the standard scoring rules: on the same games, the model's log loss was 0.664 and its Brier score was 0.236. Lower is better for both; a model that always predicted 50% would score 0.693 and 0.250.

How the test was run

The model was trained on earlier NHL seasons, then asked to predict every game from February 26, 2023 onward without ever seeing their results. Each prediction uses only information available before the game. The probabilities graded here come from the model alone; they are not blended with sportsbook odds, so the accuracy reflects what the model learned, not what the market already knew.

The model behind these numbers predicts each team's goals from expected goals, shot quality, rest, back-to-backs, travel, injuries, the starting goaltender and team strength ratings, and derives the win probability from the projected score. The full method is explained on NHL AI Predictions.

Frequently asked questions

How accurate is ArakIce?

ArakIce picked the winner in 59.5% of 4,304 NHL games in an out-of-sample test (February 2023 to April 2026), against 54.0% for always picking the home team. Its win probabilities were within 2.1 percentage points of the observed results on average.

What is a good accuracy for NHL predictions?

Around 58% to 62% of winners over a full season is the realistic range for a strong NHL model. Hockey has a lot of randomness, so claims of 70% or more over a full season are not credible.

Does a high accuracy mean the bets are profitable?

No. Favourites win more often but pay less. A bet is only worth placing when the model's probability is higher than the probability implied by the odds. That is why ArakIce focuses on calibrated probabilities and shows the gap with the sportsbook price, rather than just picking winners.

Are these results cherry-picked?

No. Every game in the test period is included, the model never saw those games during training, and the weak spots in calibration are published alongside the strong ones.
